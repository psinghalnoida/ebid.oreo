Part B: Platform Concepts Glossary
Plain-language definitions for the platform-specific mechanics referenced throughout this document,
alphabetical. Written in technical vocabulary (Part A), matching the rest of Sections 2 through 5.
Baton-Pass (Cascading Default) — What happens when a winning bidder doesn't pay: the win passes to the
next-highest bidder at their own price, within their own time window. This can happen up to three times before
an auction is cancelled outright. See also: Forfeiture, H1 / H2 / H3.
Bid Ceiling (150% Rule) — A safety limit stopping any single bid from jumping more than 150% above the
current price — catches typing mistakes and blocks deliberate price manipulation.
Buy-Now — A sale format where buyers make offers and the seller personally chooses who to sell to,
considering price and the buyer's rating — not necessarily the highest offer. See also: Express Auction, Easy
Auction.
CAT-LOC-VAL — Short for Category-Location-Value — the set of preferences (what you're interested in, where,
and at what price range) that personalises which listings and alerts you see. See also: Live Ticker.
Certified by Seller (CBS) — A listing where the seller has taken their own photos, by any method. Real, unedited
photos of the actual item are required — stock or generated images are never allowed. Contrast with Verified
Listing. See also: Verified Listing, Standing Review.
Crawl-Back — A recovery path for a buyer whose rating has dropped significantly — they're temporarily limited
to smaller purchases until a set number of clean transactions restores their standing. See also: Star Rating,
Shadow Banning.

Dispute Resolution — The formal process for resolving a disagreement about a specific transaction — filed by a
buyer or seller, reviewed with evidence from both sides, and decided with a stated reason. See also: Grievance
Redressal, Standing Review.
Dynamic Time (Anti-Sniping) — A rule that extends an auction by a short period whenever a bid lands in the
closing minutes, so a last-second bid can't unfairly end the auction before others can respond.
Easy Auction — A scheduled auction with a real inspection window (set by the seller, 24 hours to 7 days) before
bidding opens. The seller can choose whether results close instantly or need their approval. See also: Buy-Now,
Express Auction.
EMD (Security Deposit) — The refundable deposit — 10% of the item's value — a buyer must pledge before
placing a bid or offer. It's returned automatically unless you win and then fail to pay. See also: Forfeiture, Money
Points (PC).
EV (Expected Value) — The price a seller states as their target/asking figure in a Buy-Now listing. Buyers may
offer above or below it — it's a reference point, not a hard limit. See also: Reserve Value (RV), Buy-Now.
Express Auction — The fastest sale format — no inspection window at all. The auction only starts once exactly 3
buyers have pledged a deposit, then runs for about an hour. See also: Easy Auction, Buy-Now.
Fee Payer Election — A choice a seller makes for each Trading Session, before it opens: who pays the Success
Fee — the buyer (the default) or the seller. Once bidding starts, it can't be changed. See also: Success Fee.
Forfeiture — The loss of a buyer's deposit after they win an auction and then fail to complete payment. In most
cases the money goes to the seller as compensation; in a full cascading default, it doesn't. See also: EMD
(Security Deposit), Baton-Pass (Cascading Default).
Grievance Redressal — The process for raising a broader concern about the platform itself — separate from a
dispute about one specific transaction. See also: Dispute Resolution.
H1 / H2 / H3 — Shorthand for the highest, second-highest, and third-highest bidder in an auction at the moment
it closes. If H1 defaults, the win passes to H2, then H3. See also: Baton-Pass (Cascading Default).
KYC — Know Your Customer — the identity verification every user completes before transacting, using
documents like PAN and Aadhaar (for individuals) or company registration details (for businesses).
Live Ticker — The real-time scrolling feed showing your account balance, the status of your active bids, and new
listings matching your interests. See also: CAT-LOC-VAL.
Money Points (PC) — The platform's internal representation of deposited funds — always equal in value to what
you deposited (1:1), held as a distinct amount tied to each specific bid, not one shared spendable balance. See
also: EMD (Security Deposit).
NOC (No Objection Certificate) — A digital confirmation both sides must submit before a deal is considered
closed — the seller confirms they were paid, the buyer confirms they received the goods. See also: Settlement.
Payment Gateway Charge — A processing fee some payment methods (like cards) carry, added on top of your
deposit so the full deposit amount still reaches escrow untouched. See also: EMD (Security Deposit).
Related Auctions — Multiple listings that share a common origin (e.g., items from the same site) shown
together on one screen for convenience — while each one remains a fully separate, independent sale.
Reserve Value (RV) — The confidential floor price a seller sets for an Easy or Express Auction. The item won't
sell for less than this, and it's what a buyer's deposit is calculated against. See also: EV (Expected Value), Easy
Auction.
SaaS Admin (Super Admin) — The platform's top-level administrator, who oversees the whole system but never
personally buys, sells, or has any visibility into a live auction while it's happening. See also: Tenant Admin.

Seller Rating (sellerStarRating) — Your reputation score specifically as a seller — separate from your buyer
rating, even if you do both. See also: Star Rating.
Settlement — The final stage of a completed sale: the buyer pays the seller directly, both confirm their side
(NOC), both rate each other, and any remaining deposit is refunded. See also: NOC (No Objection Certificate).
Shadow Banning — A gradual reduction in visibility (fewer alerts, less promotion) for an account with a
persistently poor rating — not a full block, just reduced platform support. See also: Crawl-Back, Star Rating.
Standing Review — A periodic, big-picture check on a seller's overall conduct — triggered either once a year, or
sooner if enough complaints build up — that looks at the pattern, not just one incident. See also: Certified by
Seller (CBS), Dispute Resolution.
Star Rating — Your reputation score, starting at 3 out of 5, that rises with clean, on-time transactions and falls
with defaults or disputes. Buyers and sellers each have their own, separate score. See also: Seller Rating
(sellerStarRating), Crawl-Back.
Subscription Tier — The package level a Tenant operates under — CoCo Starter (free), TSX Launch, TSX Growth,
or TSX Enterprise — which determines things like API access and whether Seller-Pays is available. See also:
Success Fee, Tenant API Access.
Success Fee — The platform's own transaction fee on a completed sale — a single rate that declines as the sale
value goes up, the same for every Tenant regardless of size or negotiating power. Replaces the older term
'commission'. See also: Fee Payer Election, EMD (Security Deposit).
Tenant / Shop — An individual storefront operating on AdwitiX under its own branding — could be a bank, an
institution, or the platform's own general marketplace. See also: Tenant Admin.
Tenant Admin — The person a shop has authorised to approve listings, set local terms, and manage sellers on
that specific storefront. See also: Tenant / Shop, SaaS Admin (Super Admin).
Tenant API Access — A way for a Tenant's own systems to connect directly to the platform — pushing listings or
checking status automatically — instead of using the web portal by hand. Available on paid subscription tiers
only. See also: Subscription Tier.
Tender Auction — A fully private, invite-only sale format where the seller personally controls who can
participate and on what terms — available only through the platform's own operated shop. See also: Buy-Now.
Verified Listing — A listing where AdwitiX's own inspection team has visited the item and taken the photos
themselves. Contrast with Certified by Seller. See also: Certified by Seller (CBS).

SECTION 2: BUSINESS RULES (CONSTRAINTS)
BR-01: Discuss First & Rationale Archive
Statement: Every business rule, process flow, and technological choice must be discussed, reviewed, and
finalized with the Super Admin prior to any software development. The development agent must always halt
and raise clarifying questions rather than making unilateral assumptions on ambiguous requirements. Every
discussion and its resulting decision is preserved as a rationale record.
Logic / Rationale: Eliminates assumptions, prevents development drift, and guarantees stakeholder intent is
strictly documented. The preserved rationale additionally serves as the raw material for future FAQs and deep
user-facing documentation, since it captures not just what was decided but why.
BR-02: Patron Unique Phone Identification & Authentication
Statement: Each platform patron (buyer or seller) is uniquely identified by a single verified mobile phone
number. New users verify identity via OTP during registration before profile creation. Registered users
authenticate via a secure 4-digit mPIN. On 3 consecutive failed mPIN attempts, an SMS OTP is triggered; the user
must verify this OTP before mPIN reset or re-authentication.
Logic / Rationale: Mobile-number-as-primary-key minimizes duplicate accounts and spam bidders. OTP enforces
device ownership at registration and recovery; mPIN gives a fast, passwordless path for ongoing use.
BR-03: Indian Localization & Jurisdiction Limits
Statement: The platform's operational domain, compliance, and transactions are restricted to India. Mobile
identification is restricted to 10-digit Indian numbers with '+91'. All transactions are valued and settled in
Rupees. Application language is Indian English; location is governed by 6-digit Indian PIN codes. This is the
platform's current scope, not a permanent architectural ceiling — it is explicitly revisitable if/when international
expansion is pursued.
Logic / Rationale: Enforcing territorial boundaries ensures legal/tax compliance (GST), simplifies logistics via the
Indian postal grid, and aligns with Indian banking rails (UPI/IMPS/VAN).
BR-04: SaaS Sovereign Administrative Control
Statement: Piyush Singhal (contact: psinghalnoida@gmail.com) is the platform's Super Admin. Only the Super
Admin holds sovereign, non-delegable authority to create, edit, suspend, or delete business rules, workflows,
categories, commissions, or safety thresholds. Super Admin login uses TOTP-based multi-factor authentication
(Google Authenticator / Microsoft Authenticator compatible, via Auth0), isolated from the standard user
OTP/mPIN flow. The Super Admin's real identity (name, mobile, email) is never exposed publicly; every public-
facing surface refers to this role only as "Super Admin."
Logic / Rationale: High-value asset platforms require a definitive, authenticated administrative anchor.
TOTP/MFA via a specialized identity provider (rather than custom-built authentication) minimizes security risk
for this single, highest-privilege account without adding unnecessary engineering overhead.
BR-05: Immutable Auditing, Logical Archiving & 5-Year Retention
Statement: Every operational interaction, configuration change, and transactional action is tracked in an
append-only, tamper-resistant audit trail with microsecond-accurate UTC timestamps. True physical deletion is
prohibited. A hybrid hot/cold tiering structure is enforced: logs under 1 year old remain in the primary database
('Hot' tier); logs older than 1 year are compressed, encrypted, and offloaded to cold cloud object storage ('Cold'
tier), retained for a minimum of 5 years. A unified Log Reader API retrieves and joins records across both tiers,
exposed exclusively to the Super Admin.

BR-06: Federated SaaS Multi-Tenancy & Tenant Whitelisting
Statement: The platform operates as a federated multi-tenant SaaS. Only the Super Admin can whitelist tenants.
Each tenant is provisioned with a dedicated subdomain (e.g., pnb.ebidhub.com) or custom domain (e.g.,
www.salvagemanagers.com), instantly loading a white-labeled portal with the tenant's own branding and terms.
Buyers are federated globally — one registered identity lets a buyer log in across any tenant domain and
monitor all their activity from a single dashboard.
Logic / Rationale: Enables fast, zero-infrastructure onboarding for sellers/institutions while giving buyers a
seamless, single-identity purchasing path across every tenant.
BR-07: Salvage Asset Categorization & Scope Restriction
Statement: The platform is strictly a marketplace for salvaged, claims, surplus, repossessed, used, and
specialized assets. New retail-consumer goods (Amazon-style) are explicitly prohibited. Permitted categories:
Salvaged Claims Goods, Second-Hand/Used Goods, Abandoned Goods, Antiques, Repossessed Banking Assets,
Industrial/Commercial Surplus, Custom/Confiscated Goods, and Lost-and-Found inventories.
Logic / Rationale: Positions the platform as a trusted, institutional-grade marketplace for non-new goods,
preventing scope creep into general retail e-commerce.
BR-08: Turnover-Based SaaS Monetization
Statement: SaaS’s primary transactional revenue is the Success Fee: a declining schedule by final sale value
(2.00% down to 0.50%, minimum ₹500 + GST), fully specified in Section 6.4. This replaces the earlier flat 0.5%
figure. Who pays the Success Fee (Trader or Market Maker) is configurable per Trading Session via Fee Payer
Election (Section 6.4, BR-32); the platform’s revenue is identical regardless of that election. Fee mechanics are
settled per BR-32 and BR-34.
Logic / Rationale: Aligns SaaS’s commercial incentive directly with transaction volume. Superseding the earlier
flat 0.5% figure with a declining-by-value schedule rewards larger transactions with a lower marginal rate,
consistent with the Business Model finalized in Section 6.
BR-09: Tenant Admin Delegated Administration
Statement: Each whitelisted tenant is administered by exactly one Tenant Admin at a time — a specific person
holding a normal buyer/seller account, additionally granted a time-bound admin role by formal agreement
between the Super Admin and the tenant's company. The company designates (and may replace) whoever holds
this post; it is never self-appointed. The Success Fee is a fixed, platform-wide schedule (Section 6.4) that the
Tenant Admin cannot adjust; the Tenant Admin's discretion is limited to Fee Payer Election (Section 6.4, BR-32)
on their storefront's Trading Sessions. The Tenant Admin still reviews and approves listings, and holds exclusive
authority to upgrade a buyer to a Seller — scoped strictly to that tenant's own storefront. A seller upgraded on
one tenant has no automatic selling rights on another. If a seller's account is suspended, all of their active listings
on that tenant are instantly suspended pending review.
Logic / Rationale: Ensures tenant-isolated operations, prevents catalog contamination across tenants, and
keeps listing/seller-conduct governance close to the institution actually running that storefront.

description, category, condition, quantity, location, inspector contact) and a Sale System (the transactional

BR-11: Universal Listing Metadata & Inspector Governance

BR-12: Multi-Format Transactional Sale Systems
Statement: The platform supports four sale systems: (1) Buy-Now — seller sets an Expected Value (EV); buyers
offer at, above, or below it; seller reviews and selects by judgment, not automatic highest-price close. (2)
Express Auctions — seller sets a Reserve Value (RV); once exactly 3 distinct buyers pledge EMD, the auction is
scheduled and launched automatically; no inspection window exists at all; result is fully automatic. (3) Easy
Auctions — scheduled open bidding at RV; result may be Instant Close or Approval Required (seller's choice at
listing). (4) Tender Auctions — fully bespoke, invitation-only, seller-curated, restricted exclusively to the
Company Shop. The Company Shop is the Salvage Managers Tenant specifically — the platform's own
operational entity, not a generic label any Tenant can hold. Where Section 6 refers to “the Salvage Managers
TSX,” this is the same designation.
Logic / Rationale: Provides a flexible suite of disposal mechanisms from hyper-rapid liquidity (Express) to
maximum-value discovery (Easy) to fully bespoke concierge handling (Tender), while Buy-Now serves
negotiated, judgment-based sales.
BR-13: Listing Lifecycle, Immutability & Safe-Edit Redirection
Statement: Every listing follows: INVENTORY to PENDING_APPROVAL to UPCOMING to (seller assigns one Sale
System, generating a new unique Sale-System ID) to ACTIVE to SOLD or CYCLE-ENDED-UNSOLD. Only one Sale-
System instance may be ACTIVE per listing at a time. Once ACTIVE, no parameter can be changed directly. Any
requested change is submitted as an edit request; on Tenant Admin approval, the original listing is archived
(preserved for audit trail) and a new listing is created with a new unique ID, re-entering the lifecycle from
UPCOMING. Edit-request review is tiered: material fields (RV, EV, unit/lot basis, quantity/tolerance, category,
sale-system choice) trigger full re-review; non-material fields (description, media, minor detail) receive lighter-
touch review. Any rejection — of an original listing, an edit-request draft, or a re-submission — requires the
Tenant Admin to select a reason from a closed list and log it; the rejection and its stated reason are visible to the
seller.
Logic / Rationale: Ensures catalog immutability during active sales, protects active bidders from bait-and-switch
changes, and preserves a full audit trail across every relisting attempt. Mandating a logged reason on every
rejection fixes accountability for the decision and gives the seller a documented, contestable basis rather than
an unexplained refusal.

BR-15: Sovereign Isolation — Super Admin Non-Participation
Statement: The Super Admin occupies an absolute, non-participatory regulatory role — strictly forbidden from
acting as Buyer, Seller, Tenant Admin, Inspector, or any other platform participant, and structurally barred from
listing assets, attaching Sale Events, or placing bids/offers under any circumstance. Sovereign authority is
restricted to whitelisting tenants, appointing/removing Tenant Admins, auditing logs, and executing emergency
global freezes.
Logic / Rationale: Guarantees neutral governance and eliminates any insider-trading risk at the platform-
operator level.
BR-16: Double-Blind Market Privacy & Visibility Matrix
Statement: During live bidding on any competitive-bid format (Easy, Express, Tender default): a bidder sees only
their own running price and H1/not-H1 status, never other bidders' identity or amounts. The seller sees price
movement only, no bidder identity, during the live event. After close, the seller sees only the H1 winner's score,
name, and details; the Tenant Admin sees the full bid history plus H1/H2/H3 identity and amounts (not the full
roster). In Buy-Now, full double-blind anonymity applies: the buyer does not know the seller's identity until EMD
is pledged (which unlocks seller identity/location/inspection access), and the seller does not know the buyer's
identity until they accept an offer. The Super Admin is fully blind to all of this during live events, by deliberate
design — access unlocks only after close, same as every other viewer, preserving platform-operator neutrality.
Logic / Rationale: Deters bidding cartels, enforces objectivity in offer selection, and guarantees that even the
platform operator has no live informational edge while a sale is in progress.
BR-17: Dual-Track Patron KYC Verification
Statement: Individual KYC requires Full Name, PAN, Aadhaar (masked/tokenized), Date of Birth, and Occupation.
Corporate/Organization KYC requires CIN, GSTIN, Company PAN, MSME Registration, UDYAM Number, Company
Type, Industry, and optional Annual Turnover/Employee Count. Required document uploads: PAN Card, GST
Certificate, Aadhaar Card, Certificate of Incorporation, Board Resolution, Power of Attorney, Cancelled Cheque,
MSME Certificate. Any specific field's mandatory/optional status, or the addition/removal of a field, remains
subject to BR-01 — this schema is a living structure, not permanently frozen by adoption. Transitioning a
patron's KYC Status to Suspended requires the reviewing admin to select a reason from a closed list (e.g.,
document mismatch, expired ID, failed registry check) and log it; the patron is notified of both the suspension
and its stated reason.
Logic / Rationale: Guarantees legally verified participants, prevents shell-company operation, and protects
sensitive data (e.g., Aadhaar) via tokenization. A mandatory, logged reason for suspension fixes accountability
for an adverse action and gives the patron a documented basis to correct or contest it.

BR-19: Polymorphic Role Mapping
Statement: A single verified Party (legal entity) may simultaneously hold one or more of the following

BR-20: Super Admin Credential Isolation
Statement: Super Admin credential changes (TOTP re-enrollment, email/mobile update) are strictly barred from
the standard SMS-OTP/mPIN self-service channels used by all other patrons. These changes route exclusively
through the isolated TOTP/MFA path established in BR-04.
Logic / Rationale: Eradicates SIM-swap and front-end injection risk against the platform's highest-privilege
account.
BR-21: Asset-Level Transactional Role Exclusion
Statement: A Party bound to a Listing as Surveyor, Yard Inspector, or Physical Custodian (BR-11) is
algorithmically blocked from placing bids, submitting Buy-Now offers, or acting as Financier on any active Sale
Event mapped to that specific listing — even though the same Party may hold buyer/seller roles freely
elsewhere on the platform.
Logic / Rationale: Prevents collusive evaluation reports and conflicts of interest between physical assessors and
financial participants.
BR-22: Tenant-Scope Bidding Suspension
Statement: The moment a registered buyer is promoted to Tenant Admin for a specific tenant, their profile is
immediately blocked from submitting bids or Buy-Now offers within that specific tenant's storefront. They
remain fully free to bid on any other, independent tenant's storefront where they hold no administrative access.
Logic / Rationale: Guarantees institution employees with administrative oversight cannot bid on their own
company's liquidations, while preserving their ability to transact elsewhere.
BR-23: Category-Location-Value (CLV) Matching
Statement: Buyers are shown all listings by default, with persistent tools to fine-tune their feed: preferred
categories, comfort inspection locations (specific states, or 'All India'), and a dual-slider budget range (min/max).
This preference set drives personalized match suggestions and notification triggers, including the Live Ticker's
'sales of interest' feed (BR-48).
Logic / Rationale: Maximizes trading efficiency by filtering noise for active bidders and empowering self-curated
deal sourcing.
BR-24: Shipping Attribution & Cost Transparency

Fixed Cost or a Variable (distance-based) Cost. Shipping is always optional for the buyer — they may lift the
asset themselves at no shipping cost. CLV budget-matching (BR-23) is evaluated strictly on the listing's Basic
Cost, never the shipping-inclusive landed cost.

BR-25: EMD Escrow Isolation
Statement: To comply with RBI regulations prohibiting unlicensed private digital wallets, the platform holds
Earnest Money Deposits as segregated, per-pledge escrow holdings — never a pooled, freely spendable wallet
balance. A buyer's deposited funds are 'unpledged' until committed to a specific bid or offer; the moment they
are pledged, that amount becomes a distinct, ring-fenced holding tied to that specific transaction, released or
forfeited only according to that transaction's own outcome. What a buyer sees as their account 'balance' is a
display total of unpledged funds plus their currently pledged holdings — not a single fungible pool they can
freely draw down. No automatic cross-channel or cross-pledge fund pooling exists anywhere on the platform.
Separately, and independently of this segregation, PC/Money Points is never used to pay for the underlying
goods themselves — 100% of every sale value is settled directly between buyer and seller, offline; PC only ever
secures a bid commitment, never functions as the payment instrument for a purchase.
Logic / Rationale: Ensures regulatory compliance, avoids classification as an unauthorized Prepaid Payment
Instrument (PPI), and secures buyer/seller funds from operational commingling risk. Both safeguards are
deliberate and independent: per-pledge segregation ensures no general-purpose spendable balance exists,
while PC never being the instrument that pays for goods (only ever a security deposit) further distinguishes it
from a wallet's core function. This framing is the platform's working position and should be confirmed by
qualified legal counsel before being relied upon as a final compliance determination.
BR-26: Multi-Channel EMD Routing
Statement: EMD validation routes differently per sale format: Tender Auctions — manual offline bank transfer
into the platform's corporate escrow account; no gateway; manual refunds. Express Auctions and Buy-Now —
buyer's choice of Virtual Account Number (VAN) instant transfer or Credit Card authorization hold. Easy Auctions
— VAN only; credit card authorization is barred, to avoid chargeback exposure on high-velocity liquidations.
Logic / Rationale: Routes each channel through the transaction speed/risk profile appropriate to its format,
keeping high-value legacy Tender transactions fully manual and insulated.
BR-27: EMD Baseline Calculation Protocol
Statement: A flat 10% EMD is mandatory to place any bid or offer, calculated as: 10% of the seller's Expected
Price (EV) for Buy-Now; 10% of the listing's Reserve Value (RV) for Express and Easy. No bid or offer is accepted
without this baseline being pledged and available, checked live on every single bid, not only at close. Where the
buyer's chosen payment method incurs a Payment Gateway collection charge, the buyer is charged that amount
in addition to the stated EMD, calculated and disclosed at the point of payment, so that the full, undiminished
10% baseline is what actually lands in escrow — the collection charge is never deducted from the EMD itself.
Logic / Rationale: Establishes a uniform entry threshold for commitment, deterring frivolous bidding while
satisfying escrow-holding requirements. Grossing up for the Payment Gateway's own collection charge keeps
the escrowed amount mathematically exact at all times, which every downstream calculation (top-up, forfeiture,
refund, commission deduction) depends on — a silently reduced escrow balance would compromise all of those
figures.
BR-28: Cascading H1 to H2 to H3 Baton-Pass & Forfeiture
Statement: If an auction closes above Reserve, the winning H1 bidder must top up their EMD to 10% of the
actual closing value within a fixed window: 2 hours (Express) or 24 hours (Easy). If H1 fails to pay, they default
and forfeit their EMD; the baton passes to H2, who now acts as H1 at H2's own bid price, with an equivalent
window (Express: hours 2-4; Easy: 24-48hr). If H2 also defaults, the baton passes to H3 under the same terms
(Express: hours 4-6; Easy: 48-72hr). If H3 also defaults, the auction is cancelled outright — it is not automatically
relisted. On full cascade failure (all three defaulting), the seller receives zero share of any of the three forfeited

BR-29: Buy-Now EMD Adjustment & Identity Masking
Statement: If the final negotiated Buy-Now offer accepted by the seller is higher than the original Expected

Logic / Rationale: Protects platform commission collection, prevents off-platform collusion, and protects user

BR-30: Format-Specific Inspection Rules
Statement: Express Auctions have no inspection window at all — buyers commit sight-unseen, consistent with
the format's high-velocity design. Easy Auctions and Buy-Now instead use a fixed 60-minute post-approval
edit/grace window (BR-14) rather than a multi-day physical inspection period; buyers rely on listing photos,

Logic / Rationale: Aligns each format's information availability with its own risk profile — Express prioritizes
speed above all; Easy/Buy-Now provide a short parameter-correction window rather than a physical inspection

BR-31: Success Fee & Fee Payer Election
Statement: The Success Fee is a single, platform-wide, non-tenant-adjustable schedule, declining by final sale
value from 2.00% down to 0.50% (minimum ₹500 + GST), fully specified in Section 6.4. It replaces the earlier flat-

Auctions; Tender Auctions remain excluded and follow the CoCo Concierge terms instead (Section 6.2). By

Logic / Rationale: Ensures a zero-friction entry barrier for sellers by default, funding the platform via buyer-side

BR-33: Dual-Sided NOC & Mandatory Rating Settlement Gate
Statement: Upon a successful sale, the buyer pays 100% of the sale value directly to the seller, offline, either
after the seller ships the goods or the buyer lifts them from the yard. The platform never touches this 100%

confirming goods received; (3) the buyer submits a mandatory star rating of the seller; and (4) the seller submits

buyer and the Success Fee is instead deducted from the seller's proceeds.
Logic / Rationale: Guarantees each party independently attests to the fact only they can verify (money received
/ goods received), and guarantees the Star Rating system is fed by genuine transaction volume rather than
optional, low-participation feedback.
BR-34: Forfeited EMD Allocation
Statement: Outside a full cascade-failure scenario (BR-28), when a buyer defaults on their balance EMD, the
escrowed EMD is fully forfeited. Standard allocation: the Success Fee (BR-31) that would have applied under that
session's Fee Payer Election is deducted first; the remainder passes to the seller as compensation for the failed

Logic / Rationale: Compensates the seller for a genuinely failed deal in the ordinary case, while the cascade-

BR-36: Rating Governance — Human-Approval-Gated Downgrades
Statement: Star rating upgrades apply automatically. Downgrades, however, require explicit review:
downgrades between 5.0 and 2.1 stars must be approved by the Tenant Admin; downgrades to 2.0 stars or

appeal right for the affected user.
Logic / Rationale: Protects users — particularly buyers, the platform's core patrons — from retaliatory or unfair

BR-37: Rating Portability Across Tenants

Logic / Rationale: Prevents a bad-actor operator from erasing their reputation by simply moving to a new

BR-38: Crawl-Back & Shadow Banning — Rehabilitation System
Statement: Rather than an automatic hard suspension at any single threshold, a buyer whose starRating drops
below 2 stars enters Crawl-Back: they are restricted to transacting only within that tenant's own defined 'Low'
value bracket, with a defined number of consecutive clean transactions (escalating by offence count) required to
restore 3 stars. Below a further threshold, both buyers and sellers enter Shadow Banning — a graduated
suppression of platform-driven visibility (search, recommendations, ticker promotion) rather than an outright
block; the user can still manually transact if they choose. A platform-wide, non-tenant-configurable floor applies
at 1 star or below (a flat transaction ceiling), which can be raised via a published, SaaS-controlled standing-
deposit formula. A mirrored flush-out ladder applies to sellers on sellerStarRating, ending in full delisting
reserved strictly for confirmed fraud — never for accumulated ordinary poor ratings.
Logic / Rationale: Provides a real, achievable path back to good standing rather than a punitive dead end, while
still containing risk to sellers/buyers during a user's rehabilitation period and reserving permanent removal for
genuine fraud only.
BR-39: Stall Resolution — Forced Neutral Rating Mechanism
Statement: If a party required to submit a mandatory settlement rating (BR-33) fails to act, the system sends a
time-bound warning message. If they still do not respond, the relevant admin (Tenant Admin for a stalling seller;
Super Admin for a stalling buyer) force-applies a neutral 3-star rating on the stalling party's behalf, unblocking
deal closure. This forced rating is neutral, never punitive, since the admin cannot know what the missing party's
genuine assessment would have been. A single instance of this carries no consequence to the stalling party;
however, 5 such instances recorded against the same user (tracked independently per role — buyer or seller)
constitutes a pattern that itself triggers a −0.5★ rating penalty on the stalling party's rating (per the graduated
event table in BR-35). Where the missing item is a stalled NOC rather than a rating, the admin's determination is
a factual finding, not a neutral placeholder: the admin must log the specific basis relied upon (e.g., bank
statement cross-reference, direct contact with the other party, delivery record reviewed) alongside the NOC
entry, not just the resulting confirmation.
Logic / Rationale: Prevents a stalling party from indefinitely blocking the other side's settlement, while avoiding
the unfairness of fabricating either a good or a bad signal on their behalf — and still holding chronic non-
responsiveness accountable as its own behavioral pattern. Logging the investigation basis for a force-logged
NOC fixes accountability for what is, unlike a rating, an assertion of fact.
BR-40: Dispute Resolution Framework
Statement: A structured dispute mechanism covers Payment Disputes, Condition/Delivery Disputes, Non-
Lifting/Collection Disputes, Auction Rejection Disputes, Buyer Non-Response Disputes, and Standing Review — a
sixth category, distinct from the first five in that it is system-initiated rather than filed by one party against
another, with a Seller (or, symmetrically, a Buyer) as its subject rather than a respondent to a specific accuser
(see BR-61). Tender is excluded entirely, consistent with its full isolation from the platform's EMD/rating system.
A dispute must be filed within a defined window of the triggering event; both parties then have a defined
window to submit evidence; the ruling authority (Tenant Admin for seller-side/transaction disputes, Super
Admin for buyer-side disputes) then rules within a further defined window. Turnaround time targets favor the
fastest resolution the evidence allows, rather than treating any window as a mandatory minimum delay. A
Tenant Admin ruling may be appealed once, to the Super Admin, whose decision is final; Super Admin rulings
made directly (buyer-side disputes) carry no further appeal. The ruling can force-log an NOC, order EMD
forfeiture, apply a specific (non-neutral) rating consequence to the at-fault party, apply a Standing Review
outcome (BR-61), or dismiss the claim. Every ruling must include a stated rationale identifying the specific
evidence relied upon, not just the outcome — this rationale is what any appeal is actually reviewed against.
Patterns of repeated, baseless dispute-filing are tracked as their own behavioral signal, and feed into Standing
Review as one of its own triggers.

BR-51: Consent Capture & Audit Trail
Statement: Every meaningful consent point on the platform is captured as its own discrete, timestamped,
auditable event — not inferred from a single signup-time acceptance. This applies to: (a) initial acceptance of
the Terms of Usage and Privacy Policy at registration; (b) consent to KYC data collection and processing; (c) a
mandatory, specific acknowledgment shown to the buyer immediately before every EMD pledge, stating the
exact forfeiture consequence applicable to that bid, which the buyer must explicitly confirm before the pledge is
accepted; and (d) re-consent, required before further platform use, whenever the Terms of Usage or Privacy
Policy are materially amended. Each consent event is logged with the user's identity, the specific version of the
terms shown, a timestamp, and — for per-pledge consent — the specific bid/transaction reference, forming part
of the platform's immutable audit trail (BR-05).
Logic / Rationale: A general, one-time acceptance of the Terms of Usage is a weak evidentiary basis if a specific
forfeiture, chargeback, or dispute is later contested — a buyer can plausibly claim they never turned their mind
to the specific risk of that specific bid. Requiring a fresh, specific acknowledgment at the moment of each EMD
pledge creates a contemporaneous, transaction-linked record of informed consent, which is significantly more
defensible in a dispute, a chargeback representment, or regulatory review. This also directly supports the
platform's Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDP) compliance posture, which requires consent to be
specific, informed, and evidenced, not merely implied.
BR-52: Chargeback Mitigation & Recourse
Statement: Card-funded EMD is held as an authorization hold, not captured, for as long as operationally possible
— capture occurs only at the point money genuinely needs to move (settlement fee deduction or a confirmed
forfeiture event), minimizing the window in which a completed charge (and therefore a chargeback) exists at all.
Buyers are strongly directed toward VAN/UPI bank-transfer funding as the default, with card funding offered as
a secondary option, since bank-rail payments carry no card-network chargeback mechanism. A chargeback filed
against a legitimate, already-approved forfeiture (BR-28/34) does not extinguish the buyer's underlying
obligation; the platform retains the right to pursue recovery, and such a chargeback is itself treated as a serious
account-integrity event, escalated for review rather than accepted at face value.
Logic / Rationale: Chargebacks are a documented fraud/dispute vector specific to card networks; delaying
capture and preferring bank-rail funding both reduce the attack surface directly, while the recourse clause and
consent trail (BR-51) together give the platform a genuine evidentiary basis to contest an illegitimate chargeback
rather than simply absorbing the loss.
BR-53: Tax Deduction at Source (TDS)
Statement: As an e-commerce operator facilitating sales between Buyers and Sellers, the platform deducts Tax
Deducted at Source on the gross amount of facilitated sales, in accordance with Section 194-O of the Income Tax
Act (or its successor provision), at a confirmed rate of 10%. Any future change to the prescribed statutory rate is
confirmed with a qualified tax advisor before implementation.
Logic / Rationale: TDS obligations under Section 194-O attach to platforms facilitating e-commerce transactions;
the Super Admin confirmed the applicable rate directly at 10%, and the platform computes and records it on
every settlement at the point of completion, so the obligation is never silently missed once real transaction
volume begins.

BR-58: Statutory Books & Audit Trail Exportability
Statement: The platform's immutable audit trail (BR-05) is maintained in a form that is exportable to support

Logic / Rationale: The audit trail already captures every transactional event needed for statutory bookkeeping;

BR-59: Two-Tier Listing Media Standard — Verified & Certified by Seller
Statement: The choice between the two media designations always rests with the seller — never mandated by
the Tenant or SaaS Admin for any category or value bracket. 'Verified': ADWITIX's (or the Tenant's authorised)
inspection team physically examines the item and captures the photographs themselves, in-app, with GPS and
timestamp data at the moment of capture, per the mechanism in BR-45. The seller pays for this inspection, at
the rate published in the platform's Fee & Charges Schedule. 'Certified by Seller' (CBS): the seller captures the
media themselves, by any method or device, with or without GPS/timestamp data — the only absolute
requirement is that the photographs are genuine images of the actual item; stock photography, catalogue
images, or any created/generated image standing in for the real item is strictly prohibited, with no exception
absent an approved Tenant Media Waiver (BR-60). The applicable tier is displayed clearly to buyers on the
listing.
Logic / Rationale: A single, rigid capture standard is disproportionate for the majority of sellers and listings;
splitting into two tiers preserves a genuinely strong, independently-verified guarantee where it matters most
while keeping the default seller experience simple (kiss). The one absolute CBS prohibition — no stock or
generated imagery — is the single line that most directly protects buyers from outright fabrication, and is
deliberately kept as the only hard technical rule at this tier.
BR-60: Tenant Media Waiver
Statement: A Tenant may apply to SaaS Admin for a waiver from the CBS no-stock/no-catalogue-image
prohibition (BR-59), scoped to one specific category, where item-specific photography is not meaningful to that
category's business reality (e.g., bulk/siloed commodities, rental space, surplus goods sold by specification). SaaS
Admin reviews and decides, logging the stated business justification and the decision's own rationale. An
approved waiver is valid for 12 months from its grant date, after which it lapses automatically unless SaaS Admin
affirmatively re-reviews and renews it — inaction results in lapse, not continuation. Independently of the 12-
month cycle, SaaS Admin may revoke a waiver immediately if a serious issue arises. Every listing published under
an active waiver must visibly disclose that its images are representative, not item-specific.
Logic / Rationale: Balances the CBS prohibition's fraud-prevention purpose against categories where it is simply
inapplicable, without weakening the rule generally — the waiver is narrow (one category, one tenant), actively
reviewed (not a permanent exemption), and always disclosed to buyers, so trust in the platform's general 'real
photos' standard is not diluted by a legitimate, disclosed exception.

integrity for every EMD, commission, and settlement transaction (BR-25 through BR-34); (b) sub-second real-

with Indian payment/banking regulation, avoiding any unlicensed pooled-wallet (PPI) classification (BR-25).

4.2 Real-Time Bidding Engine
• Node.js (Express) + WebSockets/Socket.io handles high-concurrency real-time bid broadcasts for every live
auction (Easy, Express) and the Live Ticker (BR-48).
• Redis holds live bidding-room state — current high bid, leading-bidder reference, anti-sniping countdown
clock — so high-frequency read/write during an auction's final seconds never touches the relational
database directly.
• Anti-sniping is implemented as a database-lock pattern: lock the auction record, validate the incoming bid
against the current price plus increment, record it to PostgreSQL, extend the close time if the bid landed
inside the trigger window, and broadcast the new state to every subscribed client immediately.
4.3 Data Storage & Persistence

(EMD pledge, forfeiture, commission split, refund) requires strict ACID transactions.
• The database enforces the same Listing-vs-Sale-Event architectural split as BR-10 — a Listing and a Sale
Event are separate entities, with each Sale Event carrying its own immutable reference number.

4.6 Audit Trail & Long-Term Retention
• Hot tier: the append-only PostgreSQL audit_log table, holding all events under 1 year old.
• Cold tier: a scheduled monthly job Gzip-compresses and encrypts records older than 1 year and writes them
to a dedicated Google Cloud Storage bucket with a 5-year lifecycle policy.
• A unified Log Reader service queries both tiers and merges results on one timeline, exposed exclusively to
the Super Admin's audit console (BR-05).
• Tamper-evidence: the audit_log table is write-only at the application layer — no update or delete grant
exists for any application role, including Super Admin's own operational database credentials. Each audit
record includes a cryptographic hash chained to the previous record's hash, so any retroactive tampering —
including by someone with raw database access — is detectable.
• Cold-tier immutability: archives in Google Cloud Storage are written with object-lock/retention-lock
enabled, making them immutable for their retention period even against a compromised or malicious
admin credential.
• Control separation: the audit/logging pathway is administratively separate from the primary application
database — access credentials for the audit trail are distinct from, and not a superset of, general database
administration credentials.

• All auction timing (Dynamic Time triggers, extension windows, EMD deadlines, cascading baton-pass

Admin and is itself logged as an audit event.
• This protects the integrity of every timing-dependent mechanic on the platform against clock manipulation
— a documented risk vector for online bidding systems, since an unsynced or tamperable server clock could
otherwise be exploited to view bids before close or to manipulate auction-extension timing.

conducted before go-live and at a minimum annual cadence thereafter.

• Audit findings and remediation status are tracked to closure, not just reported.
• This satisfies a maker-checker principle: the team that builds the system should not be the sole party
certifying its security.

revenue is identical either way — only who it is billed to changes, letting a Market Maker on a paid tier
match a 0%-buyer-premium competitor (e.g. BAANKNET) without the platform discounting its own take.

SECTION 7: AX KNOWLEDGE & CHRONICLE FRAMEWORK
Status: Phase 1 — AX Chronicle ("Capture. Certify. Preserve.") — is the Trading Session Chronicle (7.10),
active and built into this release. Phase 2 — AX Intelligence ("Understand. Learn. Recommend.") —
covers everything else in this section as design reference (7.11), the same way Section 5 defers
Procurement and Market Intelligence. Adapted from the AX Knowledge & Chronicle Framework concept
paper (v1.0).
7.1 Vision
Every transaction creates information; every decision creates knowledge. Most organisations preserve
the transaction and lose the reasoning behind it — inspection reports in one folder, approvals in emails,
photographs on mobile phones, audit observations in separate files. The AX Knowledge & Chronicle
Framework turns operational information into a permanent, searchable, trustworthy record that
supports management, auditors, regulators, and future decision-makers — not just recording that an
event happened, but preserving the complete lifecycle of the decisions behind it.
7.2 Guiding Principles
• Facts before Opinions — facts are recorded first, interpretation comes later; AI may assist
interpretation but never creates facts.
• Human Accountability — technology assists, people remain accountable; only authorised
Contributors certify information.
• Single Source of Truth — information exists once; every report, dashboard, audit, and certificate
references the same underlying data.
• Preserve Everything — no information is discarded for not appearing in the final report; working
information stays archived, certified information stays visible.
• Knowledge over Documents — reports are outputs; the knowledge behind them is the asset.
• Governance by Design — transparency is embedded into workflows, not dependent on individual
behaviour.
• Information Creates Value — every transaction should increase the organisation's knowledge.
7.3 Information Hierarchy
Information is organised into progressively richer business objects: Case → Asset → Lot → Event →
Transaction → Evidence → Observation → Finding → Decision → Chronicle → Dossier → Knowledge
Vault. Three of these already exist under different names: Lot is this document's existing Listing (Section
1); Event is this document's existing Sale Event / Trading Session; Transaction is this document's existing
Settlement. Evidence already exists as the media captured by PR-09's upload pipeline, not yet formally
classified as such. Case, Asset (as a lifecycle entity spanning multiple Lots — distinct from BR-47's lighter
Related Auctions grouping), Observation, Finding, Decision, Chronicle, Dossier, and Knowledge Vault are
new concepts introduced by this framework; only the Chronicle level is in Phase 1 (AX Chronicle) scope
(7.10).
7.4 Chronicle Hierarchy
Different stakeholders need different levels of reporting, so the framework supports multiple Chronicle
types, each building on the last:

• Event Chronicle (Trading Session Chronicle) — the history of one Auction, Tender, Buy Now, or
Negotiation. This is Phase 1 — AX Chronicle (7.10).
• Lot Chronicle — the complete lifecycle of a Lot across multiple Events. Phase 2 — AX Intelligence.
• Asset Chronicle — the history of all Lots belonging to an Asset. Phase 2 — AX Intelligence.

• Enterprise Chronicle — portfolio-level reporting for management. Phase 2 — AX Intelligence.
7.5 Contributors
Rather than departments, the framework organises certification authority around Contributors — Asset
Owner, Surveyor, Valuer, Technical Expert, Event Manager, Finance, Compliance, Logistics — each
certifying only the information they own; AI compiles, humans certify. This overlaps with, but is not
identical to, BR-21's existing bound asset-level roles (Surveyor, Yard Inspector, Physical Custodian).
Whether Contributors extends BR-21's role system or is built as a separate certification layer is explicitly
undecided and needs its own dedicated design discussion before any Contributor-related capability is
scoped into a build — not part of Phase 1.
7.6 Information Classification
Every record is classified as one of: Fact, Observation, Decision, Action, Recommendation, Exception,
Evidence, Approval, Conclusion, or AI Insight. This classification is what makes a Chronicle more than a
document dump — it is what lets a Dossier (7.7) filter the same underlying knowledge differently for
different audiences. Full adoption of this taxonomy across every existing decision point (dispute rulings,
Standing Review outcomes, KYC review decisions, rating events) is Phase 2 (AX Intelligence); the Phase 1
(AX Chronicle) Trading Session Chronicle (7.10) draws directly from existing tables and the audit trail
rather than requiring this taxonomy to exist first.
7.7 Dossiers
A Dossier is a purpose-built information package presenting only what's relevant to its intended
audience, while every Dossier still draws from the same single source of truth. Named Dossier types:
Audit Dossier, Legal Dossier, Claim Dossier, Management Dossier, Buyer Dossier, and Compliance
Dossier — this last one is this document's existing KYC verification packet (BR-17/18's dossier review,
already built), reclassified as one instance of this general Dossier concept rather than a separate,
unrelated thing. Building the other Dossier types is Phase 2; they depend on Chronicles existing first.
7.8 Access & Visibility
Information is protected along three dimensions — Role (who is accessing), Scope (which organisation,
case, or marketplace), and Permission (what level of access) — plus a classification tier: Public, Internal,
Confidential, Privileged, Regulatory, or Court Access. Different users see different views of the same
underlying record without the record being duplicated. Phase 1's (AX Chronicle) Trading Session
Chronicle uses the existing role-based access already governing its source data (the Seller and Tenant
Admin who can already see the Sale Event) plus one deliberate exception — the QR-linked public
verification page (7.10) — rather than building this full six-tier model now. The full classification system
is Phase 2 (AX Intelligence).

7.10 AX Chronicle (Phase 1) — the Trading Session Chronicle
Capture. Certify. Preserve.
The first Chronicle built is the Event Chronicle, scoped concretely as a Trading Session Chronicle: a
system-authenticated report generated automatically the moment a Sale Event's Settlement completes,
downloadable by the Market Maker (Seller) and the Tenant Admin. It contains:
• What was listed — the Lot's category, condition, Reserve Value, Expected Value, and description,
as recorded at listing time.
• What happened, chronologically — a timeline drawn from the existing BR-05 hash-chained audit
trail: listing approval, bids or offers placed, any material edits, close, and settlement.
• The result — final price, winner status, and settlement outcome.
• Participation and bidding — how many distinct parties participated and the bid or offer
progression, shown as counts and amounts only — never buyer names, mobile numbers, or Party
IDs, matching BR-16's existing double-blind masking exactly as it already applies elsewhere in this
document.
• Improvement achieved — the final price against the Reserve Value.
• The transaction — TDS (BR-53), Success Fee, and payout, as already computed by Settlement.
• Transparency and privacy — a plain statement of how BR-16's double-blind mechanism kept
participant identity masked while the process itself remained fully auditable.
The report's narrative is template-based, not AI-authored (7.9) — real, computed data filled into fixed
prose, so generation has no external dependency. It is authenticated, not merely generated: certified via
a content hash tied to the existing BR-05 audit trail, a unique reference number, and a QR code. The QR
opens a public, token-based digital verification page — reachable only with the exact unguessable token
embedded in the QR, not by ordinary login — showing the certified PDF, the Lot's photographs and
documents, the relevant audit-trail excerpt, and a hash-match confirmation that the certified copy
hasn't been altered. Once certified, a Chronicle is immutable; a later correction creates a new version
rather than editing the original, the same principle BR-13 already applies to Listings.
